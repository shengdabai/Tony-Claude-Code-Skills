# Editorial system

## Channel promise

Position Zturns as a real Chinese builder who lets overseas viewers understand a changing China through body, streets, tools, and firsthand experiments. The recurring experience is:

`real person × real setting/action × firsthand attempt × understandable explanation`

AI is a research, translation, visualization, and editing assistant. It is not a substitute for Tony, the scene, the experiment, or the judgment.

## Decision gate

Only develop an idea when all three answers are yes:

1. Can Tony connect it to a real experience, action, place, conversation, or observable detail?
2. Does it contain Tony's own judgment, uncertainty, or reaction rather than generic information?
3. Can a viewer without Chinese context understand what changes for them after watching?

If one answer is no, narrow the claim, add a filming task, or skip the note.

## Format selection

Choose `long` when the source has a story or experiment plus at least three connected beats and a meaningful payoff. Target 6–8 minutes, normally 1,300–2,400 Chinese characters of spoken body copy.

Choose `short` when the source has one surprising contrast, one concrete observation, or one answerable question. Target 35–60 seconds, normally 140–360 Chinese characters of spoken body copy.

For a long video, add two standalone Shorts. A Short must have its own hook, tension, payoff, and closing line. It must not be a clipped paragraph that depends on the long video.

## Attention architecture

Use this sequence without sounding formulaic:

1. **Honest hook**: an observable contrast, specific question, or personal surprise in the first two sentences.
2. **Why Tony cares**: one brief first-person scene or mistake.
3. **Escalation**: show two or three concrete beats, each changing the viewer's understanding.
4. **Payoff**: answer the opening question and say what Tony now believes or will test next.
5. **Conversation CTA**: ask for one specific experience or disagreement. Never beg for likes before delivering value.

Optimize for viewer choice, watch continuity, satisfaction, return visits, and trust. Do not describe any format as algorithmically guaranteed, “对新人一视同仁”, “必火”, or certain to gain subscribers.

## Bilingual verbatim design

- Produce two complete alternative recording versions: `中文逐字稿` and `English verbatim script`. Do not call the English version subtitles.
- Write the Chinese version first to preserve Tony's original meaning and speaking habits. Retain a small number of useful English technical terms when that is naturally how Tony speaks.
- Rebuild the English version from the same evidence instead of translating sentence by sentence. It may reorder beats so an English-speaking viewer gets tension, context, and personal relevance earlier.
- Keep factual scope identical across languages. The English version must not introduce a product test, place, quotation, statistic, or certainty that the source and Chinese version do not support.
- For a global Zturns upload, treat the English script as the primary direct-to-camera version. Treat the Chinese script as an alternative Chinese cut, dubbing reference, or meaning anchor. Do not record both full versions into one video unless a bilingual format is explicitly chosen.

### Idiomatic English standard

- Open with a concrete surprise, disagreement, or question within the first 15 seconds. Avoid a formal topic announcement.
- Use short, speakable sentences and natural contractions: `it's`, `don't`, `I've`, `that's`, `here's`.
- Explain China-specific context when it appears; never assume viewers know a Chinese app, payment habit, holiday, city routine, or policy.
- Prefer spoken transitions such as `Here's what I realized`, `That sounds impressive, but...`, `The real test is...`, and `I may be wrong, so I'm going to test it` when they fit Tony's meaning.
- Avoid translated-essay language: `with the development of`, `as we all know`, `in today's society`, `more and more people`, `very convenient`, `let us discuss`, and long strings of abstract nouns.
- Avoid borrowed creator slang or aggressive sales language that Tony would not naturally say. An accent is fine; unnatural wording is not.
- Give the viewer a reason to care before background exposition. End with one specific question that invites experience or disagreement.

## Trust and evidence

- Prefer “我试过 / 我看到 / 我当时以为 / 我现在更倾向于” over authority theater.
- Separate personal observation, interpretation, and external fact.
- Put dates, prices, subscriber counts, policy thresholds, market-share claims, proper nouns, and superlatives in `发布前核验` unless verified during the current run.
- Correct obvious transcription errors, but list uncertain corrections for Tony to approve before recording.
- Never claim Tony used a product, visited a place, interviewed a person, or obtained a result unless the source says so.
- Remove student names, private conversations, identifiable minors, addresses, credentials, IPs, and internal file paths from publishable copy.

## Filming guidance

Favor Tony on camera and real B-roll. Suggest only footage he can actually capture or already has. Use AI visuals for clarification, not as the identity of the channel. For the first month, treat roughly 60% Tony/action, 25% real B-roll, and at most 15% AI graphics as a planning heuristic, not a platform rule.
